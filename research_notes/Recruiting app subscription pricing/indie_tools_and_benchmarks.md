## Q1. Self-serve recruiting tools and their 2025-2026 prices (monthly, yearly, lifetime), plan contents, free tiers, annual discounts

### Takeaway
Self-serve, AI-email-centric recruiting apps cluster tightly at **$10-$50/month**. The most common paid tier is **about $15-$35/month**, and published yearly plans run **about $80-$350/year**. Almost all of them have a free tier that caps AI emails or coach cards. The incumbents NCSA and SportsRecruits sit far higher, at several hundred to several thousand dollars a year, and they hide their prices. FieldLevel is the one large incumbent with transparent pricing ($29/$49/$79 a month, about 20% off yearly).

### Inferences
- **Annual discounts implied by published prices** (my arithmetic: 1 − yearly ÷ (12 × monthly)):

  | Tool | Monthly | Yearly | 12× monthly | Implied yearly discount | "Months free" equivalent |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | AthletesGoLive Premium | $9.99 | $99.99 | $119.88 | 16.6% | ~2 |
  | ProZpects | $19.99 | $199.99 | $239.88 | 16.6% | ~2 |
  | ProductiveRecruit (llms.txt figures) | $29 | $24/mo × 12 = $288 | $348 | 17.2% | ~2 |
  | FieldLevel Silver / Gold / Platinum | $29 / $49 / $79 | $278 / $470 / $758 | $348 / $588 / $948 | 20.1% / 20.1% / 20.0% | ~2.4 |
  | ProductiveRecruit (pricing-page figures) | $39 | $349 | $468 | 25.4% | ~3 |
  | Inbox Athletics | $34.99 | $299.99 | $419.88 | 28.6% | ~3.4 |
  | REP Recruiting Edge (community) | $49 | $399 | $588 | 32.1% | ~3.9 |
  | RecruitReach (AI add-on) | $9.99 | $79.99 | $119.88 | 33.3% | ~4 |
  | SportsRecruits (reported, unofficial) | ~$99 | ~$399 | ~$1,188 | ~66% | ~8 |

  Among direct self-serve competitors, the **median implied yearly discount is about 20%**, with a range of roughly 17-33%. "Two months free" (about 16.7%) is the single most common pattern. SportsRecruits' reported ~66% is an outlier that pushes families to annual billing.
- **Published yearly price points** for direct competitors cluster at **$80, $100, $200, $280-$300 and $350**. Monthly points cluster at **$9.99, $14.99-$19.99, $24.99-$29 and $34.99-$49.99**. A product with AI email, follow-up and reply tracking, and a national coach list competes most directly with NextCommit ($19.99/$49.99), SIGND ($29/$49), Inbox Athletics ($34.99 / $299.99 a year) and ProductiveRecruit ($29-$39 / $288-$349 a year).
- **Tier gating patterns in the market:** free tiers limit **AI email volume** (NextCommit 10/week, SIGND 3 AI emails/month), **coach cards/contacts** (SIGND 10 coach cards), or **who can be messaged first** (FieldLevel and SportsRecruits free plans). Paid tiers scale by email volume (College Recruit AI 1,000 vs 5,000), and some add a **Family tier at about $49/month** (SIGND).
- **Lifetime plans:** no recruiting tool in these results advertised a lifetime price. Only the one-time **coach-list** products ($10-$20 per sport/division) are one-time purchases.
- **Seasonal or short-term passes:** none of the tools found sold an explicit 3-month or "recruiting season" pass. Vantage's limited-time $12 offer and University Athlete's $100-off code show that **time-boxed promos**, not seasonal SKUs, are the norm.

## Q3. Consumer/prosumer subscription benchmarks (2024-2026): annual discount, price points for student/education apps, yearly-plan conversion, and seasonal/short-term plans

### Takeaway
Subscription benchmarks disagree depending on how they measure. Comparing one app's own monthly and yearly plans, the common discount is about **17-20% ("two months free")**. Comparing app-wide medians, a yearly plan costs about **3-3.5× a monthly plan**, roughly a 70-75% "discount" (Adapty). Education apps have the **highest yearly median of any category (about $44.99, RevenueCat 2026)** and a monthly median of about **$8.38-$9.99**. Annual plans retain far better (about 34-36% vs about 7-14% after a year) and dominate subscription counts in North America (43% yearly share). Education has among the **lowest** download-to-paid conversion. No benchmark on seasonal or 3-month plans was found.

### Cited Findings

**Annual-plan discount norms**
- RevenueCat: "most consumer products offer annual plans at a **17% discount** relative to the annualized price of monthly plans". It warns that discounting the annual plan too much "may sacrifice revenue from folks who would have likely paid more". The search summary attributed this to the **2024** State of Subscription Apps; it may instead come from RevenueCat's annual-plans blog post. — [RevenueCat SOSA 2024](https://revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps-2024); [RevenueCat: Annual subscriptions pros and cons](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/annual-subscriptions-apps-pros-cons/)
- Adapty (2025 data): a median annual plan costs "just **3x** the price of a monthly plan globally", so annual subscribers save **75%**. Adapty also says **Education apps discount more than any other category**. — [Adapty State of In-App Subscriptions 2025 (PDF)](https://uploads.adapty.io/state_of_in_app_subscriptions_2025.pdf); [Adapty Education benchmarks](https://adapty.io/blog/education-app-subscription-benchmarks/); [RocketShip HQ summary of Adapty](https://www.rocketshiphq.com/adapty-subscription-app-benchmark-2025-summary/)
- An older third-party analysis of 100+ subscription apps found annual plans discounted **35-50%** vs monthly (**undated, likely pre-2024**). — [Alexandre (Substack): Benchmarking pricing of 100+ subscription apps](https://alexandre.substack.com/p/-benchmarking-the-pricing-strategy)
- SaaS/web billing (Recurly): the **most popular percentage discount is 16.7%**, which corresponds to "two months free" on an annual plan. "One month free" works out to 8.3%. — [Recurly: SaaS benchmarks for subscription plans](https://recurly.com/research/saas-benchmarks-for-subscription-plans)
- A ProfitWell figure, relayed secondhand by Monetizely: the industry-standard annual discount is **10-20%, averaging 16%**, across 6,000+ SaaS companies. **Not verified in a primary ProfitWell/Paddle publication.** — [Monetizely: Why annual billing discounts work](https://www.getmonetizely.com/articles/why-annual-billing-discounts-work-better-than-you-think-a-revenue-game-changer-for-saas)
- Paddle's annual-plan guidance uses a one-month-discount example and argues the case for offering annual plans "with just 10% of customers switching"; it gives no benchmark discount size. — [Paddle: Annual plans](https://www.paddle.com/resources/annual-plans)
- Adapty reports **Utilities** barely discount: only 1.2% of paywalls show a discount (another Adapty page says 1.4%). — [ppc.land on Adapty 2026 report](https://ppc.land/95-of-app-subscription-revenue-goes-to-top-10-adaptys-2026-benchmark-report/)

**Typical price points (overall and Education)**
- RevenueCat SOSA **2026**: median **yearly price rose to $34.80 (from $31.60)**; **weekly ($5.99) and monthly ($10) stayed flat** across all categories. Based on 115,000+ apps and $16B+ revenue. — [RevenueCat SOSA 2026](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps)
- RevenueCat SOSA 2026, Education: **highest yearly median of any category at $44.99** (2.2× Travel's $20). Monthly medians across categories range **$4.99-$9.99**, with Education/Health/Social highest at **$9.99**. — [RevenueCat SOSA 2026 Education](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps-2026-education)
  - **Conflict:** Airbridge's 2026 table gives Education a **monthly median of $8.38** and a **weekly median of $5.99**. — [Airbridge: Subscription app pricing by category 2026](https://www.airbridge.io/en/blog/subscription-app-pricing-by-category-2026-benchmark)
- Adapty 2025 global medians: **$7.48/week, $12.99/month, $38.42/year**. Another Adapty page says the median successful app prices its annual plan at **$29.99**, likely a different sample. — [RocketShip HQ summary of Adapty](https://www.rocketshiphq.com/adapty-subscription-app-benchmark-2025-summary/); [Adapty reports](https://adapty.io/reports/)
- Adapty Education: annual-plan LTV-type revenue per subscriber **about $52 at Day 0, growing to about $62 by Month 12**; weekly starts at about $8 and grows to about $48; monthly trails both. — [Adapty Education benchmarks](https://adapty.io/blog/education-app-subscription-benchmarks/)
- Adapty regional: European apps charge **29-39% more** than North American ones; Germany leads monthly plans at $17.19 and Japan leads annual at $54.59. — [ppc.land on Adapty 2026 report](https://ppc.land/95-of-app-subscription-revenue-goes-to-top-10-adaptys-2026-benchmark-report/)

**Annual vs monthly mix and retention**
- RevenueCat **2025**: yearly plans are the largest share of subscriptions in **7 of 12 categories**. Health & Fitness is 67% yearly and Travel 65%, while Gaming is 78% weekly. By region, **North America has a 43% yearly share** (Asia-Pacific 47%). — [RevenueCat SOSA 2025](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps-2025)
- RevenueCat **2026** (revenue view, via search summary): Productivity earns **91% of revenue from monthly plans**; Health & Fitness earns **59% from annual**. Annual plans range **28-44%** across categories, and lifetime plans are most common in **Gaming (18%)**. — [RevenueCat SOSA 2026](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps)
  - **Conflicting secondary claim:** a Medium summary says weekly plans now generate **55.5%** of all subscription revenue, with annual at **22.5% (down from 29.2%)**. Not confirmed on RevenueCat's pages. — [Medium: State of App Monetization 2026](https://chuvak-pavel.medium.com/state-of-app-monetization-2026-key-trends-from-revenuecat-and-adapty-8bdc23a4bb6f)
- One-year retention: **13.8% of monthly** subscribers are still active after a year vs **33.9% of annual**. The underlying dataset is unclear. — [RevenueCat: Annual subscriptions pros and cons](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/annual-subscriptions-apps-pros-cons/)
- RevenueCat 2025: **cheap annual plans retain up to 36%** of users after a year vs **6.7% for high-priced monthly plans**. Example: a $5/month meditation app retains more users than a $15/month one. — [RevenueCat SOSA 2025 launch post](https://revenuecat.com/blog/company/the-state-of-subscription-apps-2025-launch)
- RevenueCat 2026 (via heise): about **20% of canceled monthly customers return within a year**, while **95% of canceling annual customers never return**. RevenueCat also warns annual plans can mask weak retention through "delayed churn". — [heise.de on RevenueCat 2026](https://heise.de/-11310636)

**Conversion (trials, paywalls, education)**
- RevenueCat 2025: **Education has some of the lowest Day 35 conversion rates**, "likely due to high upfront engagement requirements". Over 80% of Education trials last 5-9+ days. — [RevenueCat SOSA 2025](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps-2025)
- RevenueCat 2026: Education download-to-trial is **6.5%**, and the iOS median download-to-paid is **3.1%** (behind Health & Fitness at 3.5%). — [RevenueCat SOSA 2026 Education](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps-2026-education)

### Inferences
- **Two different "discount" measures exist, and they should not be mixed.** Within one app, the norm is a 17-20% annual discount (RevenueCat's 17%, Recurly's 16.7%, and the recruiting competitors' 17-20% median). The cross-app median ratio is about 3-3.5× (Adapty's "75% savings"; RevenueCat 2026 $34.80 yearly ÷ $10 monthly = 3.5×; Education $44.99 ÷ $9.99 = 4.5×). The second measure reflects mobile apps that sell cheap annual plans and pricier monthly plans as separate decoys. It is not a guide to the discount a single product should set.
- Recruiting apps already price **2-5× above the education-app medians** (about $20-$35/month vs an Education median of about $8-$10). That fits a high-stakes, time-bound family purchase, and it is consistent with Adapty's finding that Education LTV skews to annual plans.
- Recruiting is **seasonal and finite** (a few years of high school, with peaks around contact periods), so the "delayed churn" and "95% of annual cancelers never return" dynamics matter. An annual plan captures a full recruiting cycle up front, while monthly subscribers are likely to churn after a burst of outreach. That argues for a meaningful but not steep yearly discount (about 20-35%) to pull families onto annual billing. This is an inference; no recruiting-specific churn data was found.
- The startup's direct competitors' yearly discounts (17-33%) sit in the same range as the general SaaS and consumer benchmark (17-20%), with AI-email tools (Inbox Athletics 29%, RecruitReach 33%) discounting more heavily.

### Gaps
- **Seasonal, 3-month or quarterly plans:** no benchmark data on their share or conversion was found in RevenueCat or Adapty summaries. No recruiting tool found sells an explicit "season pass".
- **Education-specific trial-to-paid median** and the **Education annual-vs-monthly revenue split** were not available from search summaries. The full RevenueCat and Adapty education pages could not be fetched because revenuecat.com was blocked.
- **Primary ProfitWell/Paddle discount benchmark** was not found; the 10-20% / 16% figure is secondhand.
